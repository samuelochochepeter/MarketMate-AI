# Validation workbook

Status: planned research. No customer interviews or usability tests have been conducted for this package.

## Problem statement to test

Owners of small Nigerian businesses with basic daily sales records and active social pages may struggle to turn those records into a clear campaign plan because the information sits in separate tools and requires interpretation. Test this statement before treating it as a finding.

## Persona hypothesis

An owner-manager selling one main product, using a laptop or shared computer for periodic record review, and Facebook/Instagram for enquiries. They need understandable metrics and enough time to act on them. Age, gender, income and ability to pay remain unknown. The local Python setup may itself be a barrier.

Decision criteria above are proposed pilot criteria, not programme grading requirements or statistically representative market estimates.

## Neutral interview questions

1. Tell me about the last time you planned a week's social posts.
2. Which records did you consult, and can you show an anonymised example?
3. What did you do when two metrics suggested different actions?
4. How did you judge whether the campaign worked?
5. What tools or paid services have you tried, and what happened?
6. What would prevent you from using this workflow on your current device?

## Consent script

I am testing a course project. Participation is optional and you can stop at any time. Please use only fictional or anonymised business records. I will record task completion, time, errors and anonymous feedback. I will not record customer identities or publish your business data. May I proceed?

## Usability tasks

Task A: Find total revenue and explain whether it is profit.

Task B: Identify which format you would test for awareness and which for leads. Explain the difference.

Task C: Build a content prompt with your own audience and identify one constraint.

Task D: Explain what MAE means and why the model result does not prove a promotion causes demand.

## Business model canvas (hypotheses)

| Element | Proposed starting point |
|---|---|
| Customer segments | Owners selling one main product with basic sales records and active social pages |
| Value proposition | A clear route from existing records to a measurable campaign plan |
| Channels | Voluntary demonstrations through local business communities and referrals |
| Customer relationships | Assisted onboarding followed by periodic support |
| Revenue streams | Trial a subscription only after a useful pilot and pricing research |
| Key resources | Maintained software, verified prompts, consented data and support capacity |
| Key activities | Data checks, model evaluation, user support and content quality review |
| Key partners | Willing pilot businesses and local enterprise or training communities |

## Illustrative monthly unit economics (not validated)

Test price: NGN 5,000 per business per month. Illustrative variable cost: NGN 1,500 per business per month, including support allowance. Monthly contribution before fixed costs: NGN 3,500. Illustrative fixed cost: NGN 35,000/month. Contribution break-even: 10 paying businesses. This excludes taxes, founder salary and equipment unless included in revised assumptions.

Illustrative CAC: NGN 7,000. Contribution payback: 2 months, assuming retention and costs hold. If an average account stays six months, contribution LTV would be NGN 21,000 before fixed costs. None of these figures comes from paying customers. Replace with measured acquisition, retention and service costs before making a viability claim.

## Pilot roadmap

Week 1: five interviews, device-access check and anonymised-data feasibility.

Week 2: observed usability tasks and prompt-quality evaluation.

Week 3: improve the workflow, compare another forecast baseline and retest.

Week 4: make a continue/change/stop decision based on evidence. Publish no revenue or growth claim without supporting records.
